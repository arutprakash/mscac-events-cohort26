
# Toronto Islands Event Proposal Plan

## 1. Event Summary

**Working title:** Toronto Islands Exploration Day  
**Location:** Toronto Islands, departing from the Jack Layton Ferry Terminal  
**Group size:** 30 participants  
**Format:** Guided walking tour with independent island exploration, lunch, and optional recreation  
**Proposed Date:** 4th September 2026, Friday (19 out of 29 voted this date)
**Planned duration:** 8:30 AM to approximately 3:20 PM  
**Primary objective:** Provide a well-organized day trip that combines Toronto Islands history and scenery with time for food, beach access, and optional activities.

The proposal is based on the pricing and itinerary in `toronto-islands-quote.xlsx`. The core experience is a round-trip ferry journey followed by a Toronto Island walking tour. The schedule currently uses Centre Island as the arrival point and includes a walk toward Ward's Island before returning from the ferry dock.

## 2. Objectives and Success Measures

1. Deliver a safe, punctual, and inclusive full-day group outing for all 30 participants.
2. Give participants a guided introduction to the islands, including the Centre Island gardens and the route toward Ward's Island.
3. Allow enough unstructured time for lunch, beach time, and optional activities without putting the return ferry at risk.
4. Keep the event within the quoted baseline of $2,387.10, subject to confirmation of current prices and any additional services.
5. Achieve at least 90% participant satisfaction, with no missed ferry departures, unresolved safety incidents, or preventable food/accessibility issues.

## 3. Proposed Program

| Time | Activity | Delivery notes |
|---|---|---|
| 8:30 AM | Meet at Jack Layton Ferry Terminal | Registration, headcount, ticket distribution, emergency-contact check, and briefing. |
| 8:45 AM | Group boarding preparation | Move the group through the terminal together and confirm nobody is separated. |
| 9:00 AM | Ferry to Centre Island | Use the scheduled ferry confirmed during final planning. Keep the group together and identify the event leads. |
| 9:20 AM | Arrive at Centre Island | Final headcount, orientation, washroom stop, and regroup before walking. |
| 9:30-11:00 AM | Centreville Amusement Park and gardens walk | Begin the guided walking portion. Confirm whether the tour includes admission or only guiding before booking. |
| 11:00 AM-12:00 PM | Boardwalk walk toward Ward's Island | Use a steady group pace, with a lead and a rear marshal. Include short scenic stops without blocking public paths. |
| 12:00-1:00 PM | Lunch | Provide packed lunches or use an island cafe. Keep dietary and allergy requirements with the event lead. |
| 1:00-2:30 PM | Beach and optional activities | Offer beach time as the default. Bike, canoe, or Gibraltar Point Lighthouse options are subject to availability, pricing, weather, and participant ability. |

The 3:00 PM return ferry is a planning target, not a guarantee. The organizer should confirm the exact ferry timetable close to the event date and define a backup departure if the group misses the planned sailing.

## 4. Scope of Included Services

The workbook's baseline quote includes:

- 30 adult round-trip ferry tickets at $9.57 per person, for an estimated $287.10.
- 30 Toronto Island walking tour places at $50 per person, for an estimated $1,500.00.
- Food allowance of $20 per person for 30 people, for an estimated $600.00.

The workbook does not show a cost allowance for bike or canoe rentals, amusement-park admission, venue rental, permits, dedicated first aid, transportation to the ferry terminal, gratuities, accessibility support, or contingency. These items must be confirmed before the budget is treated as final.

## 6. Planning and Booking Workplan

### 6-8 weeks before the event

1. Confirm the preferred event date, rain date, participant list, age eligibility, and meeting instructions.
2. Contact the Toronto Island walking tour provider to confirm availability for 30 people, tour duration, route, accessibility, cancellation terms, and the exact inclusions.
3. Confirm ferry schedules, group-ticket procedures, current adult pricing, and the best departure and return sailings using the Toronto ferry references in the workbook.
4. Decide between packed lunches and island-cafe service. Collect dietary restrictions and allergy information before ordering.
5. Obtain approval for the baseline budget and identify who can authorize additional costs.

### 3-4 weeks before the event

1. Make the ferry and tour bookings once timing and cancellation terms are acceptable.
2. Confirm whether Centreville, gardens, Ward's Island, the beach, and Gibraltar Point are all accessible and appropriate for the group route.
3. Check whether any activity requires a separate reservation, waiver, supervision ratio, or participant fee.
4. Prepare a participant information sheet with meeting location, schedule, footwear/clothing guidance, lunch details, accessibility contact, emergency number, and return procedure.
5. Assign event roles and prepare a printed or offline copy of the participant roster.

### 1 week before the event

1. Reconfirm every booking, ferry sailing, tour start time, meal order, and weather-dependent activity.
2. Recheck the weather forecast, water conditions, heat or storm advisories, and any island or ferry service alerts.
3. Send the final itinerary and require participants to acknowledge the meeting time and ferry dependency.
4. Prepare tickets, name list, emergency contacts, first-aid supplies, charged phones, portable battery, water, and weather backup materials.
5. Publish the go/no-go decision and the cancellation or rescheduling message process.

### Event day

1. Arrive at the terminal by 8:15 AM so the event team is ready before the 8:30 AM meeting time.
2. Check in participants, record late arrivals, distribute or validate tickets, and complete the first headcount.
3. Use a lead coordinator and rear marshal during every group transfer.
4. Repeat headcounts at the ferry terminal, Centre Island arrival, lunch departure, activity departure, and return ferry boarding.
5. Record incidents, substitutions, extra costs, and attendance for the post-event report.

### Within 3 business days after the event

1. Reconcile invoices and actual spending against the $2,387.10 estimate.
2. Send a short participant survey covering logistics, tour quality, food, accessibility, and value.
3. Document lessons learned, incidents, missed assumptions, and recommendations for future island events.

## 7. Roles and Responsibilities

- **Event lead:** Owns the schedule, budget, vendor contacts, go/no-go decision, and final escalation.
- **Registration and finance lead:** Manages attendance, tickets, payments, receipts, and dietary records.
- **Route lead:** Coordinates with the walking-tour guide and keeps the group on the approved route and timing.
- **Rear marshal:** Remains with the last participant, watches crossings and pace, and reports headcount status.
- **Welfare and accessibility lead:** Handles medical, mobility, dietary, heat, and other participant needs confidentially.
- **Communications lead:** Sends pre-event instructions and manages day-of updates if ferry or weather conditions change.

For 30 participants, use at least two internal group coordinators in addition to the professional tour guide. Increase staffing if the group includes minors, participants requiring close assistance, or multiple optional activities.

## 8. Safety, Accessibility, and Contingency Planning

### Ferry and group control

- Treat ferry capacity and sailing times as operational constraints. Arrive early enough to handle ticketing and boarding queues.
- Keep a current roster and use a buddy system for all transfers.
- Establish a meeting point on both sides of the ferry journey and a procedure for anyone separated from the group.
- Carry the ferry operator's service-alert contact details and the event lead's phone number.

### Weather and environment

- Set a go/no-go threshold for thunderstorms, high winds, extreme heat, poor air quality, or other official advisories.
- Provide a rain and heat plan: weather-appropriate clothing guidance, water reminders, shade breaks, and the option to cancel outdoor activities.
- Do not make beach, canoe, or bike activities mandatory. Replace them with a shorter sheltered or low-risk program element when conditions require.

### Accessibility and welfare

- Ask participants privately about mobility, dietary, medical, communication, and other access requirements during registration.
- Confirm the ferry, route, washrooms, lunch location, and tour format can accommodate the group before payment.
- Plan rest points and avoid splitting the group across inaccessible or optional routes.
- Keep emergency contacts and relevant health information restricted to staff who need it.

### Missing participant or medical incident

1. Stop the group at the nearest safe point and notify the event lead.
2. Confirm the last known location and contact the missing participant using the agreed procedure.
3. Call emergency services when there is an immediate threat to health or safety.
4. Keep the remainder of the group supervised while one designated lead coordinates assistance.
5. Record the incident and notify the appropriate organizer after the event.

### Missed ferry or vendor disruption

- Identify the next available ferry before departure and retain enough schedule buffer to use it.
- If the tour provider is delayed or unavailable, switch to a pre-approved self-guided route only if it remains safe, accessible, and within the approved scope.
- If lunch service fails, use the packed-lunch or participant-purchase fallback identified during planning.
